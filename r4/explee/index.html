Summary

  • The picture is the only variable. 81% of Explee's 436 ads use the same primary text, 70% the same headline ("Get your first 3-5 clients free") and 88% the same button ("Get offer"). They test images, nothing else.
  • Winners are lo-fi comparisons with a price anchor (whiteboard T-chart: 83 days, 4 of the top 7 impression slots; red vs green split: slot #2) and memes drawn for one exact pain (two doors: 78 days; reaction comic: 28 ad ids).
  • Borrowed famous memes and poster parodies died fast: 'Left Exit 12' lasted 8 days, Keep Calm 4 days, Uncle Sam 3 days. Only 13 of 138 designs ever reached 40 days; 97 died inside 2 weeks.
  • Since 31 Oct: 76 new live ads. The big pushes are founder tweet screenshots (15 live) and the clay 'Mindset 101' metaphor (8 new). An "AI employee in your Slack or Teams" test died in 3 to 9 days.
  • For Headcount: 19 static concepts on their proven formats, all with the farm of 40 visible. Build these 8 first: HC-E01, HC-E03, HC-E04, HC-E05, HC-E08, HC-E07, HC-E10, HC-E09.

How Explee runs Meta

  • One offer everywhere: "$30 in credits" to try it, "Get your first 3-5 clients free". The offer never changes, so every result is about the image.
  • Clone the winners: a winning image is re-uploaded into many ad ids (the whiteboard image sits in 21 ad ids, red vs green in 24, the reaction comic in 26). Most copies are stopped after 2 to 9 days; the best copy keeps running.
  • Kill fast: 47 ads launched since 31 Oct have already stopped; 47 of the 81 ads live on 31 Oct are gone. November kills by format: Repeated-line text wall (motion static) (16), Founder tweet screenshot (16), Breaking-news chyron (4), Two-panel reaction comic (2), Whiteboard T-chart (2), Typewriter torn-paper note (1).
  • Lo-fi over polish: whiteboards, post-its, newspapers, stick comics and clay figurines outlast slick SaaS cards.
  • Ride Claude's name: "Claude now finds your buyers", "Powered by Claude", "I'm sorry, Claude can do WHAT?!". The product is pitched as something Claude can now do.

Honest view

Genuinely strong

  • One-second comprehension: '$2,000/mo vs Free' needs no reading.
  • The enemy is familiar (Google Ads, building another feature), not a rival brand the viewer has never heard of.
  • Real-object media (whiteboard, post-its, newspaper, clay) look like content, so the thumb stops before the brain says "ad".
  • Memes are drawn for their exact pain, so the joke is the pitch.
  • Strict testing: fixed copy, fixed offer, many images, fast kills, winners cloned.

Weak or risky

  • Made-up social proof: invented tweets with fake view counts, a fake G2 review card, an invented growth chart. A policy and trust risk; we never copy it.
  • Fake scarcity: "only 9 left, gone tonight", "you have until September 30". Off-limits for Headcount (no dates or deadlines).
  • Tiny text in the red vs green and table formats is unreadable at feed size.
  • Sprawl: 138 designs in about 4.5 months, most dead inside 2 weeks. Many look like volume, not ideas.
  • Everything leans on "$30 free". A paid product like ours must make the price feel tiny instead.

Why it works on a cold audience

  • It names a pain the viewer already feels (no clients, paying for clicks) before it names the product.
  • It shows the cheaper path in one glance: a price next to a bigger price.
  • It asks for one tiny action (drop your link, $30 free) with no setup talk.
  • Borrowed trust from Claude fills the gap of an unknown brand.
  • Lesson for us: lead with one concrete nerve (salary, agency bill, idle Claude), show the farm, keep the ask small.

Headcount AI plan: 19 statics on Explee's proven formats

Clone the format, never the ad. Same layout idea and meme template; our words, our product UI, our art. No Explee image, copy or brand asset.
F0: the farm of 40 is in the headline or the picture; marketing is said early; "Claude or ChatGPT" (never "the"); any business; never Shopify.
F1 and F3: provocative hook of 8 words or fewer, one visual idea, lo-fi and real-looking, at most one support line, invite CTA such as "Meet your 40 agents".
House rules: ₹2,999 once only in the offer line; no dates or deadlines; no invented results or people; every number with its unit.
